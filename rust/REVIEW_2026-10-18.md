## Findings

* **F11. Boolean results differ structurally between macOS and Linux.**
  `resolution_booleans::imported_bodies_and_boxes_at_the_coarser_resolution`
  pins the history and result ids of fifteen imported bodies fused and cut
  with boxes at the coarser resolution. On Linux (CI's Ubuntu, Rust 1.85 and
  Windows jobs, and a Linux host (glibc 2.39): rustc 1.99.1,
  deterministically) two bodies, `consistency_ribbed` and `octant_tilt`, give
  different ids for `fuse` and `cut` in both operand orders; the other
  thirteen bodies and every `common` agree. The agent saw the symptom
  ("vertex bits differ by an ulp under glibc's hypot", `ba52da24`) and pinned
  by ids alone; the ids still differ, so the result's *structure* differs. A
  decision near the coarser resolution flips because the constructed inputs
  differ by an ulp between platform libms. The exactness contract holds per
  platform only. This is the first Boolean whose shape, not its coordinates,
  depends on the host.

  *X1's finding (the fuse dumped on macOS and on a Linux host, glibc 2.39,
  at `c0e3e35d`; every binary64 diffed).* The imported topologies agree
  bit for bit. Two libm calls in the kernel's constructions differ, macOS
  one ulp off the correctly rounded value, glibc on it:
  - `octant_tilt`: the sphere's bounds (`Solid::build_sphere`,
    `widest * x[i].hypot(y[i])`), `hypot(0x3fdc71c71c71c71d,
    0xbfbc71c71c71c71e)`: macOS `0x3fdd51e27daa88f0`, glibc and the
    correctly rounded `0x3fdd51e27daa88f1`. The body's z bounds become
    `-4.9850586808987005` / `-4.985058680898701`, so the test's box, built
    from them, has its origin's z `0xbfe84daf021d3080` / `…3078` and its
    height `0x40286fff2484431f` / `…4321`.
  - `consistency_ribbed`: the turned body's model, the cone's slope
    `k = tan(half_angle)` (`solid/imported/turned.rs`), `tan(0xbfe921fb54442d16)`:
    macOS `0xbfeffffffffffffc`, glibc and the correctly rounded
    `0xbfeffffffffffffb`. The cone's bottom radius becomes
    `58.124968647956834` / `58.12496864795683`, the body's x and y bounds
    `±55.99996948242191` / `±55.9999694824219`, the box's origin
    `-20.86558862915041` / `-20.865588629150402` and its side
    `101.59514463500983` / `101.59514463500982`.

  Injecting the Linux bounds into the box on macOS reproduces the Linux
  history and ids exactly, so these two quantities are the whole cause. No
  decision at the coarser resolution flips: both hosts give the same
  result topology and the same result ids, and only the history differs.
  The decision whose outcome differs is the history's `Unchanged` test in
  `finish` (`solid/boolean.rs`): a kept entity is `Unchanged` when its
  result geometry equals its input's bit for bit. The box's stored corner
  is the binary64 sum `o + s` (ties to even). The polyhedral engine's
  corner (`prism_model`) is the exact `o + u x + v y + w n` rounded once by
  `rational_f64`, whose ties go to the lower neighbour. On the Linux bits
  the exact sums are ties: the box top `-0.7594828645028562 +
  12.218743458886424` lies midway between `11.459260594383567` and
  `…569`, and the far side `-20.865588629150402 + 101.59514463500982`
  midway between `80.72955600585941` and `…942`. IEEE rounding gives the
  even `…569` and `…942`, `rational_f64` gives `…567` and `…941`, so those
  corners and their edges and faces become `Modified { x → x }` instead
  of `Unchanged`. On the macOS bits the sums are exact or not ties, and
  both rules agree. The same tie occurs on both hosts for the box's far y
  (`16.464256880808215` against `16.46425688080821`). The history therefore
  depends on tie coincidences in the inputs' last bits. With R13 that
  dependence is the same on every host. Making `rational_f64` round ties to
  even is a separate fix outside X1–X3.
* **F12. The pre-T1 property baseline test is host-flaky.**
  `properties_baseline::every_prism_property_is_bitwise_unchanged` asserts

## Decisions

* **R13. Kernel constructions never call platform libm.** Every binary64
  quantity the kernel constructs from its inputs (rotated frames, arc and
  circle points, imported models' frames, lengths, normals) is computed
  through the certified module's correctly rounded operations, so every host
  produces the same bits from the same inputs and the exact decisions agree
  everywhere. `certified.rs` already has Machin's π, Taylor cosine and sine,
  `atan` and an integer square root on a `2^-200` grid; a correctly rounded
  binary64 is the rational enclosure rounded once. The `Fast` tier decides
  first; the rational tier produces the bits where `Fast` cannot certify
  the rounding. The platform-allowance table in `VALIDATION.md` must end
  with no entry for a kernel construction; allowances remain only for native
  OCCT observations.
* **R14. Bitwise cross-host identity replaces the pre-T1 baseline.** After
  R13, properties are host-independent, so one committed fixture suffices
  and the per-host regeneration step at `26fc457f` is removed from CI. The
  T1 requirement "properties unchanged by the migration" was met at
  `e4adb869` and is history; the new requirement is "identical on every
  host".
* **R15. A red workflow is the next task, always.** No "done", "verified"
  or "accepted" bullet may be written while either workflow is red at that
  revision. The handoff's header states the CI state of the head commit.
  A push that turns a workflow red is followed only by commits that address
  it or revert it.
* **R16. The plan of record is a short status document.** `STATUS.md`,
  at most two pages: what exists, accepted at which revision; the open
  items; the user decisions pending; how to run the gates. Per-capability
  documents are the source of truth for contracts and evidence.
  `REVIEW_NOTES.md` is frozen as an archive (no new bullets) and
  `HANDOFF.md` shrinks to "where to pick up" with a pointer per item.
* **R17. Minimisation and replay are sharded by corpus size.** Any fuzz
  job whose replay or minimisation exceeds 45 minutes is sharded, as
  `analytic_intersections` was.

## Next development steps

After X1–X6, in order of value against the application's jobs
(`PORTING.md`'s table) and dependency on what exists.

### S10 — cancellation and work budgets

Every operation takes a budget (work units and wall time) and returns
`ComputationLimit` with its partial diagnosis when exceeded; an application
cancels from another thread. The torus near-hang (30 minutes, fixed) is
the evidence this is needed before any interactive use. Evidence: the
existing slowest fuzz inputs as budget fixtures; a fuzz invariant that no
operation exceeds its budget by more than one work unit; the DRAW adapter's
`cpulimit` mapped to it.

### S11 — the Revolve job

Profiles of lines and arcs revolved by any angle about an axis in the
sketch plane: a line yields a plane, cylinder or cone, an arc a sphere or
torus, so every surface already exists. Partial turns give planar end
caps. Ids and history follow the segment roles as extrusion does; join and
cut through S9. Evidence: `MakeRevol` histories as the native oracle, the
`revol` DRAW cases, the identity and history fixtures extended.

### S12 — the Hole job

Through, blind, counterbored and countersunk holes: a cylinder, cone and
countersink tool cut by S9 from a face point and direction, with the
hole's entities generated from the tool and named by the hole's label.
Evidence: `BRepFeat_MakeCylindricalHole` and plain cuts as native oracles;
the `feat` DRAW group.

### S13 — STEP export

AP242 writing of every body the kernel builds, with units, names and the
label-derived ids as entity names; a round trip through OCCT's STEP reader
and through the kernel's own importer must reproduce counts, properties
and validity. Evidence: the 22 authored STEP files of STEP-a read back;
the `de` DRAW group's self-contained cases.

### S14 — mirror and orientation-reversing transforms

A reflection reverses every face sense and fin sense; ids are kept
(`Modified`), as rigid motions do. Small, and it completes the Transform
job's kernel side; patterns stay in the application.

### S15 — chamfers, then constant-radius fillets

Chamfers on edges between planar and cylindrical faces are planes and
cones the kernel already has; they are the first edge-refinement feature
and exercise the tangent-chain selection through histories. Fillets need
the blend-surface decision left open in D12: decide it before S15b, with
procedural rolling-ball blends between analytic faces evaluated through
their supports (consistent with D13). Evidence: `BRepFilletAPI` native
oracles, the `blend` and `chamfer` DRAW groups (263 and 71 fully covered
data cases).

### After S15

Sweeps and lofts (procedural swept surfaces), shells and offsets, threads,
section and hidden-line queries for drawings, and spline solids in
Booleans beyond prisms, in the order the application needs them.
